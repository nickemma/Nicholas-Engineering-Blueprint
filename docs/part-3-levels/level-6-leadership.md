# Chapter 24 · Level VI — Engineering Leadership

**Weeks 39–40 + beyond · Capstone: SYNAPSE-AI (complete) · Then: continuous**

### Overview & why it matters

Everything converges. The first two weeks integrate and red-team SYNAPSE-AI into a single end-to-end demonstration; then the level never formally ends — it is the operating mode the handbook leaves you in, designed to survive contact with Penn’s workload and to carry the identity for years. Leadership here means technical leadership: your work teaches, your name means something, and you operate in public.

### Objectives / Learning outcomes

- Integrate all six systems into one coherent demonstration — the capstone that proves end-to-end capability.
- Red-team your own flagship and publish the findings — the maturity signal few engineers show.
- Transition from contributor to voice: talks, maintainership, mentorship formalized and public.
- Establish the sustainable long-run cadence that outlives the 40 weeks.

### Prerequisites

Levels I–V. Everything built now gets pointed at one demo.

### The capstone — Weeks 39–40

- **Wk39 — Integration:** SYNAPSE governs TESSERA-served agents end-to-end: an agent authenticates (synapse-ca) → leases scoped credentials (MERIDIAN-lineage store) → makes tool calls through the EMBER-based gateway → every action policy-checked and audited. All six systems, one flow.
- **Wk40 — Red-team & ship:** attack your own system — prompt-injected exfiltration, sandbox-escape attempts, audit tampering, partition during lease renewal, delegation-chain privilege escalation. Fix or document every finding. Tag SYNAPSE-AI v1. Publish the flagship essay and the red-team report.

### The signature demo

A prompt-injected agent attempts to exfiltrate a database credential — and is denied by policy, has its lease revoked by the anomaly engine, triggers an alert, and leaves a verifiable audit chain of the entire attempt. Ninety seconds. This is the demo that is simultaneously a portfolio centerpiece, a conference talk, and a product pitch.

### Beyond 40 weeks — the continuous engine

| Track | Sustained cadence |
|---|---|
| Shipping | One meaningful improvement or new component per month across the six systems — they are living projects, not frozen demos. |
| Writing | One post/month minimum; the reading log continues. The blog becomes a body of work. |
| Open source | Ladder Stage 10 — regular contributor status; ~2 PRs/month; review others; attend meetings as a voice. |
| Speaking | Local/virtual talks first (“Consistency as a per-request choice,” “Treating AI Like a User”), recorded; conference CFPs as confidence grows. |
| Mentorship | The 40+ engineers already mentored, formalized — a cohort, office hours, or a course; teaching is the deepest mastery and pure brand. |
| The handbook | Quarterly version reviews (v1.1, v1.2…); v2.0 when Penn begins; re-score the skills matrix and metrics each time. |

### Interview topics unlocked

The full stack of the identity: leading a design review, defending an architecture end-to-end, discussing failure and tradeoffs across six real systems, telling the behavioral stories (leadership, conflict, failure) from real game days and incidents — STAR format, drawn from lived work.

### Exit criteria (the blueprint’s Definition of Done)

- Six systems live, interconnected, each to Part VI standards, each demoed.
- SYNAPSE-AI capstone integration + red-team report published; flagship essay live.
- Five flagship essays + ~40 reading-log entries; 15+ merged PRs across the ladder; maintainer relationships real.
- Penn core pre-covered; electives chosen; interview-ready (~150 LeetCode, ~15 system designs, 8+ mocks, behavioral bank).
- Brand converged: one sentence, said identically by GitHub, website, LinkedIn, resume, blog, and contribution graph.
- 150+ tailored applications since Wk18; warm contacts at 20+ target companies; interviews in motion as Penn begins.

### Reflection questions

Who are you as an engineer now, in one sentence — and does every public surface say it? What did red-teaming your own system teach that building it didn’t? What is the next system only you are positioned to build — and when does v2.0 of this handbook begin?
